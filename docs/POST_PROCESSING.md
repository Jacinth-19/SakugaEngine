# AnimeSky post-processing

## Current Vulkan implementation

`engine/post/PostProcessing` owns the descriptor sets, full-screen graphics pipelines and intermediate HDR images independently of the scene renderer. The caller supplies the resolved linear RGBA16F scene and LUT, records the passes on the graphics queue, and receives an RGBA8 display-encoded image. Scene rendering includes sky and clouds, so bright sun, cloud and emissive pixels feed the same prefilter as other bright pixels. ImGui is composited afterwards. This is a scene-wide pipeline, not a baked sky texture.

1. **Optional depth atmosphere:** sample scene depth and reconstruct perspective distance in linear HDR. The default mode blends surface pixels toward the time-of-day fog colour. An opt-in single-scattering aerial mode instead integrates extinction and sun-lit in-scattering over the camera-to-opaque-surface segment using the sky transmittance and approximate second-scattering LUTs and atmospheric parameters. This pass writes a separate full-resolution HDR image before the bloom chain; clear-depth sky pixels remain unchanged. When enabled it replaces, rather than adds to, the existing toon/outline surface fog.
2. **Optional depth of field:** a separate full-resolution, nine-tap HDR gather, using scene depth to set a circle-of-confusion radius around the user-selected focus distance/range. Neighbour depths reduce cross-layer bleeding and reject geometry/clear-sky mixing. The pass accepts either the original or the fogged scene and precedes bloom. It is off by default.
3. When bloom is enabled, scene → soft-knee brightness prefilter with optional per-material and warm/cool/saturated colour-key selection applied per source tap before Karis-weighted filtering (half resolution), followed by the multilevel HDR bloom downsample and additive upsample. When supported, the main scene pass writes a separate RGBA16F selected-radiance attachment, alpha blended for transparent draws and resolved with MSAA; the prefilter can use this in place of the scene colour. Sky has an independent weight. With depth of field active, the selected-radiance buffer receives the same depth-aware gather before prefiltering.
4. **Optional separate halation pass:** wider quarter-resolution diffusion of the bright bloom buffer, composited with a red/orange bias in HDR. It is skipped unless bloom and halation are enabled; the small intermediate remains allocated. This is an artistic approximation, not a physically simulated film emulsion or individually masked lights.
5. HDR bloom/halation, optional stylised bloom-derived lens ghosts and a separate optional depth-masked sun flare → exposure → neutral/ACES/clamp tonemap → linear split-tone and saturation → sRGB encoding → display contrast and optional 3D LUT → vignette, grain and display dithering.
6. **Optional display-space finishing pass:** adjustable chromatic channel offset, luma edge darkening, optional depth silhouettes and depth-reconstructed normal edges, directional brush-like smoothing and colour quantisation with a small ordered dither. It writes a separate target and is skipped when all controls are off.
7. UI and presentation follow. The scene sky shader has its own gradient, atmosphere approximation and sun/cloud rendering.

The module rebuilds transient images/descriptors on resize or LUT replacement after the caller waits for in-flight frames. It uses dynamic rendering and explicit image barriers on the existing graphics queue, not async compute. Any future pass must document its image format, resolution, layout transitions, lifetime, inputs/outputs and ordering (linear HDR vs display-referred); do not sample an image while writing it. On Android the `Glow` control sets halation when bloom is on. In the full ImGui editor open **Post-processing (HDR)** for four creative presets and individual controls. Presets are independent of the sky palette and preserve exposure, enabled state, and the selected LUT/strength; further slider edits are allowed. Current defaults remain unchanged unless a preset is selected. The Atmospheric preset enables depth atmosphere; use the `Depth atmosphere` checkbox independently. The existing **Lighting / Scene → Fog** control adjusts density. Android also has an `Atmo` toggle in its compact panel. Its `FX` button toggles a small combination of lens ghosts, chromatic aberration, ink edges and material bloom masking where supported, plus depth of field, a sun flare and depth/reconstructed-normal outlines where depth sampling is supported. The compact `POST` panel exposes individual controls and optional pass timing; the full editor exposes the full set.

## Scope and next steps

The four presets are small tunable starting points, not reconstructions of Genshin, Honkai, Wuthering Waves or Wind Waker. They are not yet artist-calibrated on target devices. Enable **Per-material bloom mask** and edit `bloomWeight` and `bloomEmissionOnly` under the material inspector's **Rim / Emission / Highlight** group. The attachment stores selected linear HDR radiance, not a scalar gate. At emission-only 1 it contains the material's actual emitted contribution after the same surface AO, night multiplier and per-surface fog attenuation, rather than the bright reflected lighting; 0 uses the full surface colour, and intermediate values blend the two. The material weight scales this radiance before thresholding, so it changes the effective threshold as well as intensity. Transparent draws alpha-blend selected radiance over the background; sky/clouds have a separate weight, outlines select their non-emissive colour and debug lines block the selected source. Materials shared by objects share the same weight. When the HDR attachment/resolve/filter/blend capabilities are unavailable, per-material selection falls back to ordinary scene colour and colour-key bloom. The selected source is not passed through screen-space atmospheric fog or aerial extinction; when those are active it does not exactly match the post-fogged scene. Its DoF gather uses opaque depth and has the same transparent/depth-edge limitations as scene DoF. It adds a full-resolution RGBA16F attachment plus an MSAA transient attachment and, where sampled depth is available, another full-resolution RGBA16F DoF target, allocated even when the option is off. This is substantially more memory/bandwidth than the old R8 gate; measure on target devices.

The optional **Depth outline** detects depth discontinuities, including clear-sky silhouettes. **Reconstructed-normal outline** derives normals from neighbouring opaque depths, not a normal attachment, so it misses normal maps, transparent layers and some subpixel detail; MSAA uses the nearest depth sample and nonstandard projections or unsupported depth fall back to colour-only finishing. It can be costly, especially with MSAA. The painterly setting produces directional screen-space smoothing and a small procedural bristle modulation, not stable object-space or authored brush-stroke synthesis. The opt-in aerial perspective includes a bounded single-scattering approximation plus a coarse second-order term, not a full physical atmosphere; see [sky details and limitations](ANIME_SKY.md). The original lens-ghost control remains an artistic bloom-derived effect. The separate **Depth-masked sun flare** projects the procedural sun to the screen, samples five points over its disc in a 1×1 HDR source pass, and weights the source by clear-depth coverage (including individual MSAA samples) before drawing two sun-axis ghosts. It is skipped for an off-screen/behind-camera sun, unsupported depth/projection, HDRI backgrounds, disabled sun disc and night. It can run without bloom. This is an approximate occlusion mask, not a physically simulated lens: transparent objects and cloud depth are unavailable, depth and resolved colour do not correspond exactly at MSAA edges, and bright clouds/sky overlapping the sun may affect the source. Halation remains an unmasked artistic diffusion.

Depth atmosphere requires D32 depth to support both sampled and attachment usage at the renderer's selected sample count. Devices without it and nonstandard/reversed/orthographic projections automatically retain the prior per-surface fog. MSAA depth samples are read separately and the fog factors averaged; the already-resolved scene colour is then blended, so silhouette edges are an approximation. Alpha-blended geometry does not write depth and is fogged using the opaque depth behind it (or not fogged against clear sky); it is not correct per-transparent-layer atmospheric scattering. Debug lines likewise follow the opaque depth. The pass preserves clear-depth sky, not true sky/geometry segmentation at the far plane. The opt-in aerial mode uses eight midpoint samples per opaque depth sample, a 150 km segment cap, the current camera orientation, sun colour/direction and atmospheric LUT; RGB scene transmittance and inscattering are combined in HDR. Clear sky remains unchanged. The resolved scene colour is reused for each MSAA depth sample, so edges are approximate. It adds the same coarse isotropic second-scattering source as the sky-view LUT but omits higher scattering orders, ground bounce, cloud shadows and transparent-layer depths; rays are clipped at the atmospheric top and planet ground. It assumes the camera is within the atmosphere; world units are assumed to be metres. It can be much more expensive than artistic fog, especially with MSAA. Both modes fall back to surface fog on unsupported depth/projections and are not the complete physical atmosphere architecture.

Vulkan timestamp queries measure the sky draw and the whole post-processing section on the graphics queue. Opt-in **Detailed GPU pass timings** additionally measure depth atmosphere, depth of field, prefilter, grouped downsample, grouped upsample, halation, composite, and finishing; inactive groups are omitted. They are read after the frame fence without waiting for the current frame. These are approximate queue intervals, not isolated shader costs or a frame benchmark: barriers and timestamps add overhead, repeated bottom-of-pipe timestamps can change scheduling, and tile GPUs may defer work. Sky atmosphere/clouds remain one draw, so compare modes rather than treating the sky timestamp as a breakdown. No device-specific performance or visual validation has been done. Sampleable scene depth is allocated even while depth effects are off on supported devices; atmosphere and depth of field each allocate a full-resolution HDR target, material-selected bloom adds HDR targets as described above, and a full-resolution RGBA8 target is allocated for the finishing pass. Multisampled depth is stored when needed. Depth of field rejects or downweights neighbouring taps across opaque depth discontinuities and separates clear sky from geometry, but it still uses nearest opaque MSAA depth, does not correctly scatter foreground blur over backgrounds, and inherits the same transparent-depth limitations as fog. It is not physically exact. Bloom-off frames skip the half-resolution prefilter and bloom texture read, although bloom targets and the supported material mask remain allocated; quarter-resolution halation and the enabled bloom chain add GPU bandwidth; test with bloom/halation both off and on, particularly on Intel UHD and phones, before enabling more effects. The Vulkan emulator smoke checks presentation and the mobile control, not image quality or real-hardware timing. CPU tests verify profile values and pass selection without a GPU. Shader sources and their precompiled SPIR-V are both committed.
